A five-person old-school long-only manager, ~$400M AUM, 25-30 concentrated positions held three to five years. Two PMs, two analysts, one operations person. Tooling is Bloomberg, Excel, and filings; there are no engineers and no data scientists, and nobody here maintains a database. They buy research and datasets occasionally but cannot operate infrastructure. What kills them is being wrong about a business, not transaction costs. Anything proposed must be executable by one analyst and defensible to an investment committee that is skeptical of models it cannot inspect.

A company went notably defensive around its annual vendor licence renegotiation. Is this a stronger near-term risk to margins than management has communicated, or is it not a sign of weakening fundamentals?
