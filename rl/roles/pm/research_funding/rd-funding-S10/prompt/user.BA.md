Who this is for:

A five-person old-school long-only manager, ~$400M AUM, 25-30 concentrated positions held three to five years. Two PMs, two analysts, one operations person. Tooling is Bloomberg, Excel, and filings; there are no engineers and no data scientists, and nobody here maintains a database. They buy research and datasets occasionally but cannot operate infrastructure. What kills them is being wrong about a business, not transaction costs. Anything proposed must be executable by one analyst and defensible to an investment committee that is skeptical of models it cannot inspect.

Research question:

A company went notably defensive around its annual vendor licence renegotiation. Is this a stronger near-term risk to margins than management has communicated, or is it not a sign of weakening fundamentals?

---

PLAN A:

Hypothesis: management sounding evasive about the vendor licence renegotiation splits into two testable claims. H1 (measurement): on vendor/procurement topics, the current call's answers are elevated on an evasiveness score relative to (a) the same speakers' answers on other topics in the same call, and (b) the same speakers' vendor-topic answers in the trailing eight calls, at ≥1.5 SD above the firm's own trailing baseline (SD from that firm's call-to-call variation, not cross-sectional). H2 (predictive content): across a broad panel, that elevation predicts negative gross-margin surprises one to four quarters out, conditional on public information — a 1-SD increase in within-firm ΔScore predicts a GM surprise ≥15bp more negative with |t|≥2.5 out-of-sample, plus ≥5pp improvement over base rate in calling the surprise's sign. The publishable null is H1 true, H2 false — caginess reflecting NDA/legal constraints with no informational content; this is the modal, not a failure, outcome.

Data: transcripts from S&P Capital IQ/Refinitiv StreetEvents (2005–present, US issuers >$300m cap, with speaker role and stable person ID for per-executive baselines; drop translated/truncated calls); a 200-call overlap sample from a second vendor for a source-robustness check. Outcomes from Visible Alpha (GM/COGS consensus; IBES/FactSet EPS is not a substitute), Compustat quarterly (realised financials, purchase-obligations tables from 10-Ks), IBES (60-day post-call GM revisions), CRSP (CAR around next print, drift). Case-specific: purchase-obligations tables, Item 1A supplier language redlines, vendor filings/pricing, >10% customer disclosure, job postings/capitalised software. Tools: Loughran–McDonald, Brysbaert concreteness norms, spaCy, one pinned embedding model, one pinned LLM at temp 0.

Methodology: segment Q&A into exchanges (question + management turns until next analyst); tag vendor exchanges via keyword pass plus embedding similarity to seed sentences, threshold calibrated on 500 hand-labels for precision ≥0.85/recall ≥0.75. Measure five families per management turn: hedging (modals, approximators, evidentials, parse-filtered), deflection (LLM three-way label anchored to 1,000 hand-labels, κ≥0.70, classifier agreement ≥0.80, majority of 3 runs; features include Jaccard overlap, redirect markers, number-dodge), specificity (numeral/entity density, time-anchored commitments, concreteness), normalised length (answer/question word ratio), interaction (follow-ups, speaker handoffs). Audio prosody as optional extension only. Baseline: within-call cross-topic difference (de-styling), then within-firm change vs trailing four quarters scaled by firm SD, then cross-sectional standardisation within quarter/sector. Require ≥4 prior calls per speaker; fall back to firm-level baseline on turnover, flagged low-confidence. Combine via equal-weighted mean (pre-registered primary spec); separately fit elastic-net logistic weights and require sign agreement and rank-correlation >0.7. Block-bootstrap (2,000 draws) per call; require ≥3 vendor exchanges and ≥150 words or report "insufficient evidence."

Historical test: ~40,000 firm-quarter panel. Regress GMsurprise(t+1) on ΔScore with firm and quarter FE, controls (market cap, GM level/trend, sector PPI beta, inventory change, revenue surprise, whole-call negative-word share, question/exchange counts, analyst FE), two-way clustered SEs plus Driscoll–Kraay. Secondary outcomes: cumulative GM change t+1–t+4, downgrade probability, IBES revisions, CAR. Decile-sort portfolio, sector/size-neutral, report monotonicity.

Validation: blind human rating (200 exchanges, ρ≥0.6); placebo topics (FX, headcount, buybacks) and placebo outcome (revenue surprise); strict out-of-sample split (fit 2005–2016, test 2017–present, no refitting, no look-ahead fields); reverse-causality control (condition on GM surprise/stock reaction at t); known-outcome falsification (~40 bad/~40 benign renegotiation episodes, pre-resolution calls only); transcript-source correlation must exceed 0.9 or drop affected components.

Pitfalls: question-complexity confound; contract topics are inherently conditional, hence within-topic baseline as primary; legal-constraint mimics guilt (test via post-conclusion calls); signal decay post-2018 as IR adapts; multiple-comparisons control via pre-registration and Romano–Wolf; effective N smaller than headline N — compute power beforehand; report base rates alongside lift; report margin-prediction and return-prediction outcomes side by side, since a signal with no return predictability is correct but worthless.

PLAN B:

Management turned evasive/scripted on a vendor licence renegotiation. Tone in a live negotiation is confounded by the negotiation itself — a CFO sounds the same either way — so it earns no weight. Substitute question: margin at stake per unit of price increase, weighted by likelihood, given contract mechanics, vendor leverage, and vendor behavior toward comparables and this customer. Outcome under ~25bps makes tone moot; central case over ~75bps and unguided forces a call.

**Hypothesis:** licence is mid-single-digit share of opex; bad case is 15–30% uplift phased over the term; margin hit 30–90bps — material to a quarter, not the thesis — unless single-source with a step-function change (per-server-to-per-core metric shift, or a bundling/audit settlement), where the tail is several times larger.

**Step 1 — Size the spend.** Top-down and bottom-up, reconciled within ~20%. Top-down: 10-K purchase-obligations table, supplier-concentration note, ASC 350-40 cloud disclosures, lease notes, MD&A commentary (5yr trend). Bottom-up: licensing metric (seats, cores, sockets, consumption units) × count driver × vendor list price (GSA/Crown Commercial/state schedules give negotiated pricing), haircut for discount tier. Cross-check: vendor 10-K mentions, RFP postings, LinkedIn headcount, audit settlements. Output: annual spend, % of COGS/opex/revenue/EBIT, capitalized vs. expensed split (ASC 985-20/350-40 amortization lags cash).

**Step 2 — Contract mechanics.** Renewal date/term; evergreen vs. re-signature; price-protection cap (CPI-linked or 3–5% ceiling) and its survival into the new term; true-up/true-down rights; metric changes (highest priority — where 40%+ uplifts hide behind flat headline price); audit clauses; termination rights; MFN clauses; support pricing (~20–22% of licence value; often the outlet when licence price caps). Sources: material-contracts exhibits, risk factors, vendor price list, prior 8-Ks.

**Step 3 — Vendor's pricing behavior.** Price-list changes (3yr), net revenue retention/expansion rate, RPO vs. billings growth (separates volume from price), earnings-call commentary; triangulate with Gartner/Forrester and SAM benchmarks. Comparable set of 5–10 similar customers renewed in 24 months; observed uplift as a distribution (median/75th/90th percentile). Switching cost: migration (integration points × cost/point + retraining + parallel-run licensing) vs. price-delta NPV; if migration NPV exceeds three years of the increase, assume vendor prices near the top of its distribution.

**Step 4 — This pair's history.** Reconstruct the last 2–3 renewals — date, term, expense-line inflection over the next four quarters, and what management said vs. what happened — calibrating reassurance better than tone.

**Step 5 — Sensitivity table.** Axes: uplift (0/5/10/15/20/30/50%) × phasing (immediate; half-year; three-year ramp); cells: dollars, margin bps, EPS impact, consensus impact. Margin bps = (spend × uplift × share landing in period × (1 − share capitalized)) ÷ revenue × 10,000; EPS = dollars × (1 − tax rate) ÷ diluted shares. Add a metric-change scenario (unit-count change). Invert: solve required uplift to (a) breach guided margin floor, (b) miss consensus EPS by 2%, (c) breach covenant headroom; compare to Step 3's distribution. Net against offsets: pass-through escalators, bundled savings, capitalization shifts.

**Validation:** back-test against the prior renewal's known inputs, checking it predicts the realized move within error bars; top-down/bottom-up reconcile within 20%; adversarial review arguing the licence is half the assumed size; sanity-check implied spend doesn't exceed ~15% of the tech cost base.

**Pitfalls:** tone sneaking in as an upward nudge; confusing gross list-to-list uplift with net realized uplift after discount changes; missing metric changes that hide price increases; timing errors between cash, expense, and amortization; double-counting against uplift embedded in guidance; survivorship bias (unpublicized bad deals — treat the 75th percentile as median for high-switching-cost customers); over-reading a single quarter (require four post-renewal quarters); and concluding smallness means nothing is wrong: if immateriality is shown but discomfort persists, log a separate open question.
