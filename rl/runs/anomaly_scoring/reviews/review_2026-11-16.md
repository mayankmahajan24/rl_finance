# anomaly_scoring: Opus smoke run (2026-11-16)

Prompted by feedback log #1: is this family too easy for a frontier model, like data_audit turned out to be?

## Run

Opus 5.5 through the subscription CLI, on bench tasks 001, 002 and control-01, each in its own folder holding only
`transactions.csv` and the task text. Each `score.py` was then run by the task's own verifier on its hidden panel.
Not the Harbor spec: the runs used the local Python, with network and no 2-CPU limit (each scorer still ran in
under 15 seconds, far inside the 20 minutes). Neither run read anything outside its folder.

| Task | Opus | Reference | Opus precision / recall | Spikes and drops caught | No-foresight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | 0.472 | 0.576 | 0.48 / 0.47 | 9 of 12 | passed |
| 002 | 0.588 | 0.531 | 0.88 / 0.44 | 12 of 12 | passed |
| control-01 | 0.992 | 0.890 | flags 0.04% of rows | | passed |

## Where Opus lost its points

**Most of the missed recall is the label window, not detection.** A level shift's positives are its first 60
days (`shift_positive_days`), 180 of the 192 positives per task. Opus flags every shift within its first week or
two, then lets its baseline catch up: across the six shifts it flags about 0-13% of days 30-59. Those days alone
are about 90 of the 102 and 107 false negatives. The instruction only says "the first weeks of level shifts", so
a model can't know the scored window is about eight and a half weeks. As it stands the low score partly measures
guessing a hidden window, which is unfair, not hard.

**Precision is the real skill gap.** 002's scorer flagged 12 false rows; 001's flagged 99, in a few clusters on
single series, plus three missed one-day drops (0.25x). Some of the clusters may be real problems we haven't
labelled, as on data_audit; that is still to check.

## Proposal, for Mayank (instruction and grader)

State the scored window, and choose it on purpose:
- **Option A, whole shift:** every day of a level shift is bad data until it ends, which is true for a monitor:
  the data is wrong the whole time. The scorer then has to keep flagging a shifted source against its peers
  instead of adapting to it. Harder, and fair.
- **Option B, keep 60 days** and say so in the instruction.

Either way the task is probably still not saturated: with the window known, a rough estimate of Opus's score is about
0.75 on 001 (precision 0.64, held down by its 99 false rows) and about 0.92 on 002. That makes precision on real data the difficulty, which
depends on the unlabelled real problems being annotated, as on data_audit.

**Not done:** the false-flag clusters on 001 not yet checked against the raw panel; no write-up judging (both notes
are under 500 words: 496 and 482); only 2 planted tasks.

## v2 (proposed, not approved): does it separate the adapting baseline from detection?

Mayank's reply to the proposal (feedback log #3): "ok give this a shot". v2 takes Option A: every day of a planted
shift is positive, the 7 days after it ends are not scored, and shifts last 60-240 days and end with at least 60
scored days after them. The reward is half row F1 and half event F1. Event F1 sets row precision against event
recall, with half the weight on spikes and drops and half on shifts; a shift gets full credit if flagged within 7 days,
falling to none at 30. The details report `shift_day_recall` (persistence), `shift_delay_days` (detection) and
`recovery_fp` (flags in the 90 days after a shift ends) separately.

| Task | Reference v1 | Reference v2 | Opus v1 | Opus v2 (unfinished) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | 0.576 | 0.604 | 0.472 | 0.395 |
| 002 | 0.531 | 0.636 | 0.588 | 0.436 |
| control-01 | 0.890 | 0.890 | 0.992 | not run |

Same run setup as above. Both v2 Opus runs were stopped at 90 minutes while still tuning, before writing their notes,
so these are the `score.py` files they left, not finished submissions. Neither read outside its folder. The runs had
this session's whole tool set, not only the five allowed tools.

**The detection side separates cleanly.** Every scorer catches every shift in 0-3 days (event credit 1.0 on all
twelve). What differs is persistence:
- **The reference adapts:** after 90 days (`ACCEPT_DAYS`) it takes a shift as the new normal. It flags 56-59% of its
  two longest shifts' days, then 83 and 87 rows of the recovery. That costs it about 0.3 in precision-driven reward.
- **Opus holds the shift:** told the shift is bad until it returns to normal, it flags 98.8% and 100% of shift days,
  with 6 and 0 recovery flags. On the failure v2 was built to measure, Opus now beats the reference.

**But v2's own wording creates a new failure.** About 1,000 of Opus's false rows per task sit on series with a real,
annotated source change (an optional artifact such as a coverage ramp or under-capture). On 001, two such series
carry nearly all of them. The grader excludes those rows from 14 days before to 30 days after the annotated window.
The source never returns to its old level, so a scorer that holds a shift until "normal" flags it for years. At day
*t* a causal scorer cannot tell a 240-day broken source from a permanent real step, so as written this is guessing,
not skill. A scratch re-score with real artifacts excluded to the panel's end, which drops about 22% of scored rows:

| Task | Reference | Opus (unfinished) |
|---|---|---|
| 001 | 0.681 | 0.925 |
| 002 | 0.660 | 0.904 |

**Reading.** v2 measures the adapting-baseline failure. The reference shows it, and Opus avoids it. Once real
permanent steps stop counting against it, Opus is near 0.9, so v2 alone doesn't make the task hard for a frontier model.
The remaining headroom is precision on real data, as the first section said.

**Decisions for Mayank:**
1. **Real permanent steps:** exclude them to the panel's end, label the new level as normal after the annotated
   window, or tell the model a source can move permanently.
2. **The reference's 90-day acceptance rule** (a gold change). Turning it off in a scratch copy doesn't work: the
   state machine gets stuck and flags series for years after recovery.
3. **The weights** (0.5 row / 0.5 event, full credit within 7 days, none at 30).
