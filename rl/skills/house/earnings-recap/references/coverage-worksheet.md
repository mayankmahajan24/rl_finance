# Earnings recap coverage worksheet

Use this private worksheet after reading the complete call. Its purpose is to make
the evidence comparable before deciding what deserves space in the post. Keep it
compact and do not return it to the user.

## Source map

Record the materials actually available:

- earnings-call transcript and company release or presentation;
- current actuals and guidance versus Street and any separately sourced buyside view;
- prior guidance or the prior implied outlook;
- dated stock reaction and valuation when available;
- relevant house thesis or position context supplied by the user or an authorized
  research source; and
- peer facts needed for a specific read-through.

Label each entry as company disclosure, external expectation, market data, house
context, or inference. Write `not found` instead of filling a gap from memory.
When sources conflict, record both values and resolve the scope, period, or definition
before using either. Prefer primary company materials for reported facts and the full
transcript for exact management language; external sources remain necessary for
expectations and market reaction.

## Evidence ledger

Capture material evidence under these headings:

| Area | What to record |
|---|---|
| Surprise | Actual versus expectation, important operating KPI deltas, and stock reaction |
| Forward setup | New guidance versus prior and expectations, including how much of the beat or miss carried forward |
| Earnings quality | Volume, price, mix, currency, acquisition, accounting, temporary items, profit, cash, and capital intensity |
| Thesis change | The strongest evidence that changes growth, margin, retention, share, unit economics, or valuation |
| Contrary case | The best Q&A challenge, management's answer or concession, and what remains unresolved |
| Leading indicators | Orders, bookings, backlog, usage, retention, pipeline conversion, capacity, or other forward evidence |
| Strategic changes | Material transactions, products, regulation, partnerships, customer commitments, or capital allocation |
| Implications | Estimate, thesis, action, catalyst, risk, and supported peer or category consequences |
| Quotes | Exact wording, speaker role, surrounding context, and the conclusion each quote proves |

For each candidate conclusion, preserve the metric, entity, period, unit, status,
comparator, timing, causal mechanism, and strongest contrary evidence. Distinguish an
actual from guidance, a target, pipeline, design win, or aspiration. Treat a number
introduced by an analyst as an analyst premise unless management validates it.

## Complete-call sweep

After the first read, revisit every prepared-remarks theme and Q&A exchange that could
change an estimate, thesis, catalyst, risk, or portfolio consequence. Capture changes
to guidance or a long-term framework; price, volume, mix, share, retention, margin,
cash, or capacity; material transactions, products, customers, or regulation; and the
strongest analyst challenge, concession, or non-answer.

Look beneath aggregate metrics for product, segment, region, or cohort dispersion that
changes the mechanism. For each material Q&A candidate, preserve the premise,
management's response, and whether it confirms, qualifies, rejects, or avoids the
premise. A questioner's number remains a premise unless management validates it.

Compare linked operating measures instead of recording them independently. When both
volume and value are disclosed, calculate the change in value per unit when the
definitions permit it; when consolidated growth includes an acquisition or another
known contribution, calculate a labeled organic range. These bridges often reveal a
price, mix, or monetization change hidden by the headline growth rate.

Also connect evidence that is easy to miss when read exchange by exchange. Record an
explicit company-wide change in the long-term framework separately from the narrower
facts supporting it. When an acquisition or another disclosed contribution changes a
consolidated guide, preserve the inputs needed for an organic bridge. Group related
products, partnerships, and regulatory changes when they collectively create a new
usage-to-revenue path. Separate recurring operating economics from one-time accounting
or financing effects, and record the next observable proof that the new path is
converting. For a possible portfolio read-through, record the primary
demand, supply, share, pricing, or cost driver and the exposures it reaches.

## Reconciliation

Before ranking, answer four questions:

1. What changed most versus prior expectations?
2. What changes an estimate, thesis, position, catalyst, or portfolio decision?
3. What evidence most threatens that conclusion?
4. Which fact or quote would a reader need to audit the claim?

The ledger is an inventory, not the final outline. Its job is to keep a material
decision from disappearing merely because a routine scorecard fact is easier to write.
